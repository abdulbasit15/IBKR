All backtests behind the strategy & trade-management choices. Companion to configuration.html.

Read the absolute numbers with suspicion. This is a mechanical daily-bar approximation of a discretionary intraday method, on a survivorship-biased universe (only names still listed today). It enters on the close-of-breakout → next open, so it is systematically later and worse than the real discretionary trade. Only RELATIVE comparisons within one experiment (same run date, same universe) are meaningful — never compare a dollar figure in one section to a dollar figure in another (they were run on different dates / windows). Treat every "+$X" as "setting A beat setting B by this much, that day," not as expected P&L.

1 · Setup comparison — breakout vs pullback vs combined

Run 2026-10-07 · universe=us (2,080 symbols) · range=2y · risk 1%/sleeve · max-pos 30% · trail SMA20 · ext-filter 30 · partial 5d/0.5R

Which entry is more profitable — the fresh breakout or the pullback (MA continuation)? Each run in isolation, then combined.

Pullback > breakout on every metric (P&L, win%, PF, drawdown) — the only net-positive single setup. This matches Qullamaggie: the MA-pullback continuation is the lower-risk, higher-hit entry. Caveats: (1) survivorship bias flatters dip-buying more than breakouts — the real edge is smaller than +$95k. (2) The combined run's huge −$388k drawdown is an artifact: this harness has no portfolio cap, so it takes all 4,524 signals at once; the live bot's max-10 cap + quality floor never would. Conclusion used: run both as independent sleeves and let the cap pick — breakout catches fresh leaders, pullback recovers the ones joined late.

2 · Trade management — the extension ("sell into strength") partial

Run 2026-10-08 · universe=us (2,080 symbols) · range=2y · setup=breakout+pullback · ext-filter 30 · partial-min-r 0.5 · time-fallback 5d · trail SMA20 · breakeven at partial

Qullamaggie sells ⅓–½ into strength, not on a fixed day. partial_ext_adr fires the half-sell when price is ≥ N ADRs above the trailing MA (a climax, can trigger early), with the 5-day/0.5R rule as the fallback. 0 = pure time rule (the old behaviour).

4 ADRs above the MA is the sweet spot. 3 trims too early (shrinks the average winner → cuts runners short); 4.5–5 is too rare to help. The peak is sharp, so the honest read is "≈4 ADR modestly-to-strongly beats the time rule, downside ≈ baseline," not "expect +$31k live." partial_ext_adr=4 is set in all configs.

3 · Trim trigger × breakeven placement

Run 2026-10-08 · universe=us (2,080 symbols) · range=2y · setup=breakout+pullback · ext-filter 30 · partial-min-r 0.5 · trail SMA20

The two independent knobs: when to trim (time vs extension vs both) and where to put the stop after the partial (breakeven is conventional but notorious for shaking you out). be_stop_r = stop placement after the partial, in R from entry (0 = break-even, <0 = below entry/looser, >0 = lock profit; "don't move" keeps the original setup-low stop and lets the MA trail catch up).

VariantTradesWin%Net P&LPFAvg win / lossMax DD

Yes — dropping break-even adds +$38.6k even on the old time-only trim (+$12.9k → +$51.5k). The break-even move, not the trim trigger, is the single biggest drag in the whole system.

Verdict — two levers, breakeven is the bigger one. Decomposing from the current rule (time-only + break-even, +$12.9k): the extension trim adds ~+$18k, and dropping the immediate break-even adds ~+$38.6k; together ext-4 hybrid + trail-only (no BE) = +$62,898, roughly 5× the current rule. The conventional "move to break-even after the partial" is actively harmful — it stops you flat on a normal pullback, then the name resumes without you. Tradeoff: looser post-partial stops deepen drawdown (−$429k vs −$336k). A drawdown-conscious middle ground is +0.5R lock (+$47k at the shallowest DD of the winners, −$371k). Faithfulness note: Qullamaggie does say he moves to break-even — but his is discretionary/structural; the crude "snap to entry the day after the partial" is what tests badly. Trailing the MA (also his method) and leaving the stop alone tests far better.
